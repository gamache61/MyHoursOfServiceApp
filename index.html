The daily log

Every 24-hour day (midnight to midnight) is drawn on four lines. The line moves to whatever the driver is doing, and each line's hours are added up on the right. This example is a legal day.

The 4 duty statuses

  • Off duty: relieved of all work and responsibility. Meals, rest, home, motel.
  • Sleeper berth: resting in the truck's bunk.
  • Driving: behind the wheel with the truck moving.
  • On duty (not driving): pre-trip and post-trip inspections, fuelling, loading, unloading, waiting at a dock, paperwork, chaining up.

Daily limits

  • No driving after 13 hours of driving in a day.
  • No driving after 14 hours on duty in a day. On duty means driving plus on-duty, not-driving time.
  • At least 10 hours off duty every day. Off-duty and sleeper berth time both count.

The 30-minute rule

Apart from the 8 consecutive hours, the rest of the 10 off-duty hours can be spread through the day, but only in blocks of 30 minutes or more.

A 15-minute coffee stop is still a break, but it does not count toward the 10 hours.

The work shift

  • A shift starts after 8 consecutive hours off duty. No driving without that break first.
  • In a shift: no driving after 13 hours driving or 14 hours on duty.
  • No driving once 16 hours have passed since the shift started. Breaks do not stop that clock.
  • Once any of these limits is reached, take 8 consecutive hours off before driving again.

Cycles

  • Cycle 1: no more than 70 hours on duty in any 7 days.
  • Cycle 2: no more than 120 hours in any 14 days. After 70 hours on duty you need 24 consecutive hours off before driving again.
  • Reset: 36 hours off in a row (Cycle 1) or 72 hours (Cycle 2).
  • Every driver needs at least 24 consecutive hours off in every 14 days.

Hours left in the cycle

For Cycle 1, add up the last 6 days. Take that from 70 to get the hours you can work tomorrow.

Each night the oldest day drops off and its hours come back.

Example: the last 6 days add up to 62 hours, so 70 − 62 = 8 hours tomorrow.

Not covered in these drills

Sleeper-berth splitting, deferring 2 off-duty hours to the next day, emergency and adverse driving conditions, north of 60°, personal conveyance, and provincial differences. Check the full regulations (SOR/2005-313) and your carrier's policy.
